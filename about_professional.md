# Six years of professional experience.

Overall, I would describe myself as a maturing mid-level engineer, guided by the following principles:

* **Humility towards technology**

I will never say, "I've been learning long enough." I have worked on two large-scale projects in very different domains: GPU-based hardware acceleration and a full OS stack in the context of implementing a Trusted Execution Environment (TEE). Both have been tremendous learning opportunities throughout my employment, continuously exposing gaps in my knowledge while motivating me to dive deeper into subjects I had previously only been briefly exposed to.

* **Continuous learning is a must. Make use of every opportunity.**

One more language standard. One more CLI tool needed for a single task that may never come up again. Revisiting things partially forgotten after long periods of not using them. An entirely new language emerging as an industry standard. Moving to another CPU architecture. Yet another proprietary standard I have never encountered before.

These things will never stop coming. At the same time, it is easy to settle into a seemingly sufficient comfort zone. I try to avoid that at all costs.

* **Adaptation instead of an endless search for the perfectly tailored position**

I have learned not to be afraid of entering an entirely new world—whether that means taking on a different role within my team, moving to another part of a project, working with a different technology, or entering a different domain of software engineering.

I prefer low-level programming and being exposed to the internals of operating systems, but I also know that I can adapt when circumstances require it.

* **Appreciating the importance of soft skills**

Technical competence alone does not make a good engineer. Over the years, I have increasingly come to appreciate the importance of clear communication, knowledge sharing, asking the right questions, accepting criticism, and being able to explain technical problems to people with different levels of expertise.

I believe that good engineering is not only about finding the right technical solution, but also about being able to work effectively with the people involved in building it.

# Now, let's get to the specifics.

## Where I feel most comfortable

I enjoy working with low-level code, such as device drivers and kernel internals, but I appreciate any opportunity to write C/C++—from basic CLI utilities to GoogleTest-based test fixtures. I especially enjoy work that gives me an opportunity to learn and deepen my technical knowledge.

One challenge I would like to take on is becoming highly proficient at debugging complex, real-world problems, including those in particularly demanding contexts, such as bugs in custom memory management implementations.

I also feel quite comfortable working with Linux. I've been using Ubuntu professionally and Pop!_OS for personal use. The fact that Linux is difficult to truly master is something I find appealing—there is always another layer to understand.

I am interested in gaining in-depth, academically grounded knowledge through my professional work. One of my long-term goals is to dive deeper into the fields of telecommunications and electronics in general.

## Projects that I have been working on as a developer (incl. sub-projects).

* **NEO Compute Runtime.** Open-source project.
  [Repository](https://github.com/intel/compute-runtime)

  User-space driver and compute runtime for hardware acceleration using Intel GPUs. It provides implementations of the OpenCL and Level Zero APIs (part of the Intel's oneAPI ecosystem).

  [**Here you can see my contributions to the project.**](https://github.com/intel/compute-runtime/commits?author=km-nowak)

  **Interesting sub-projects:** implementing a custom ELF-based file format for combining executable code with metadata, including a human-readable representation of the latter.


* **Trusted Execution Environment for ARM-based Samsung TV platforms.** Closed-source project.

  Working with ARM TrustZone technology to implement a GlobalPlatform-compliant Trusted Execution Environment (TEE) coexisting with Tizen, acting as the Rich Execution Environment (REE).

  Developing software for both the TEE and REE worlds, including daemons, device drivers, and TEE OS internals.

  Working with physical TV development boards as well as QEMU- and Buildroot-based environments. Development and maintenance of an internal test laboratory built and configured using Labgrid.

  Working interchangeably across software engineering, testing, and DevOps responsibilities within a team of approximately seven engineers.
